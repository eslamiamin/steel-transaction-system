# 01 — Project journey and requirements

## Context and objectives

The company trades steel wholesale (B2B). Two operators work on a local network. Initial access to business data was through reports/Excel; the transaction system is intended to create a cleaner operational source for downstream analytics. The initial hosting constraint was no dedicated Windows Server for the app, so a Windows workstation hosted Streamlit while SQL Server provided central storage.

The long-term objectives are standardized transaction capture, lower manual workload, commission automation, profitability and ROI analysis. The immediate approved scope is purchase, sale, correction and supporting reports; the old process remains authoritative during parallel validation.

## Business rules

- Cash settlement: today or tomorrow; later settlement is credit. Business convention: 7% monthly credit charge, calculated manually by operators; no automatic interest or date-based classification in the application.
- Transaction type: exchange (بورسی) or non-exchange (غیر بورسی). This is distinct from pricing structure.
- Quantity and rates are manually entered. Two-rate option means two independent quantities and rates, not a second rate applied to the entire quantity.
- Office invoice number is manually entered and required by the UI; internal IDs are generated by SQL. Office number uniqueness is not enforced.
- Purchase precedes sale. Multiple sales can reference the same purchase; remaining quantity cannot become negative through the app.
- A sale displays allocated purchase cost and spread using the selected purchase's weighted average. This was an implementation assumption, not a formally approved accounting policy.
- Corrections require a reason. The sidebar actor is manually selected and distinct from the seller responsible for the transaction.
- Product selection is category → subcategory → size → product. Master data is managed in SSMS, not edited in the app.

## Scope exclusions

No automatic commission, credit calculation, payment ledger, returns, voids, FIFO, net profit, ROI or live DWH integration. Services (loading, warehousing, cutting, weighbridge) are defined but not financially linked to V1 transactions. Multi-product invoice support from the initial PoC is not present in the current one-product-per-purchase model.

## Data preparation

Whitespace and duplicate names were normalized. Counterparties acting in both roles remain in separate customer and supplier tables. A generic exchange-purchase supplier label is retained as a temporary business workaround. Product codes use TMP prefixes; incomplete specifications remain unknown rather than invented.
